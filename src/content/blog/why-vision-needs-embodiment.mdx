## The passive bottleneck

A passive observer has to account for every ambiguity upfront. Is that object in shadow or is it dark? Is the surface reflective or transparent? The model must learn an enormous range of visual variation, most of it irrelevant to actually *doing* anything.

An embodied agent gets to move. It can change its viewpoint, poke the object, watch how light falls across it from a new angle. Perception becomes active — a loop rather than a snapshot.

## What this opens up

When vision serves action, several things fall into place:

- **Disambiguation through interaction.** Uncertainty that would require millions of labeled examples can be resolved with a single physical probe.
- **Grounded representations.** Features that matter for manipulation — weight, friction, deformability — emerge naturally from contact, not from pixel statistics.
- **Efficient learning.** The agent only needs to understand what's relevant to the task, not every possible visual configuration of the world.
