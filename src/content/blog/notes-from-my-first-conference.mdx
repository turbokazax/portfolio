I presented my first poster last week. I was nervous in the way that only matters when you actually care about the work — not stage fright, but the feeling of putting something you built in front of people who know the field better than you do.

## The conversations mattered more than the talks

The talks were good. But the real value was standing next to a poster for two hours and getting asked sharp questions by strangers. A few of them are still rattling around in my head:

- "What happens when the lighting changes completely?" — I didn't have a good answer.
- "Have you tried this on a real robot, or just simulation?" — Just simulation. Yet.
- "Why that loss function?" — This one I could defend, and it felt good.

## What I'm taking home

Three things stick with me:

1. **People are generous with their time** if you've done real work and can talk about it honestly.
2. **The gap between a method and a system** is where most of the effort lives. Papers make it look clean.
3. **Good questions outlast good answers.** I left with better questions than I arrived with, which is the whole point.

I'm already thinking about what the next poster will need to show.
